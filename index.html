Broker vs. Direct Bank Approach

Choosing between a mortgage broker and a direct bank involves different approaches to loan selection. A bank will present its own range of products, whereas a broker can compare offerings from multiple lenders. This distinction influences the types of questions a borrower can ask. Instead of solely focusing on the lowest advertised interest rate, a borrower can inquire about the range of lenders considered, the assumptions underpinning the loan assessment, and the rationale behind a specific recommendation aligning with the borrower's stated objectives. Regardless of the path chosen, a thorough checklist should cover borrowing capacity assumptions, required documentation, the suitability of pre-approval, the trade-offs associated with different loan features, and potential costs that may arise before settlement or refinance completion. The typical remuneration for brokers involves commissions paid by lenders, although some may charge a fee for specific services, which must be disclosed upfront. Evidence-based discussions should focus on lender options, documented costs, loan conditions, and the justification for why a recommended loan serves the borrower's best interests.

Pre-Approval and Low Deposit Considerations

The information suggests that pre-approval for a home loan is generally valid for approximately three months, though this can vary between lenders and remains subject to final checks. It serves as a valuable planning tool, but borrowers must still satisfy requirements related to income, expenses, deposit evidence, property specifics, and lender conditions before settlement. Claims regarding low deposit options require current verification. References are made to pathways for deposits as low as 5 percent, including schemes like the Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee and family guarantor arrangements. Options for buyers with less than a 20 percent deposit often involve Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI). It is crucial to note that government schemes have specific caps and eligibility criteria, meaning a particular pathway should not be relied upon until current rules are confirmed for both the borrower and the property. Key questions include whether the pre-approval is fully assessed or merely indicative, what conditions might change after a property is selected, the applicability of LMI if a guarantee is unavailable, and which documents are necessary before making an offer.
